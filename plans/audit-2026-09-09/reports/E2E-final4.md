## 3. Findings — every one pinned to a layer. None fixed (no application code changed).

**G1 — a blind transfer by the answering party now resolves the dialog but kills the transferee.**
`P0`, `apps/engine`. The REFER is accepted, the dialog is found (F2), `routeTransferee` starts a
walk on the transferee's leg — and 2 ms later that leg ends `NORMAL_CLEARING/16` and the walk is
abandoned. This is the same seam F5 fixed for park and for `recallSharedLine`: the transferor's leg
is released while the transferee is still pointing at the bridge they shared, so the peer-ended
watcher fires. F5's `plan-walker.ts` guard (`this.deps.channel.bridgeId !== bridgeId`) cannot help
here because nothing on the transfer path clears the transferee's `bridgeId` before the transferor's
hangup — `park` does it explicitly and `recallSharedLine` was given the same `setBridge(undefined)`.
The transfer path in `apps/engine/src/calls/call-control.ts` needs the third instance of it.
Evidence: sipd `reason:"transfer_failed" detail:"the transferee could not be routed to 1202
(aborted); the routing walk ended the call"` at 20:48:56 and 20:50:14, each preceded by the engine's
`the caller's leg ended (NORMAL_CLEARING/16) while the dial was running`.

**G2 — after a shared-line retrieve the lamp still names the previous appearance, and the next
recall is therefore never armed.** `P1`, `apps/engine/src/calls/call-control.ts`
`retrieveSharedLine`. Reported unfixed in FIX-final3 and reproduced here. `shared-line-state` reads
`state:"seized"` with 1202's `heldByExtensionId` and 1202's `legId` after 1203 retrieved the call.
When 1203 then holds, no recall log line appears at all (final3's `status:"aborted"` recall is gone,
which is F5 working) — the recall is armed against a leg that is no longer the holder. Retrieve must
re-point the bucket via `lines.resume(...)` to the retrieving appearance.

**G3 — a queue callback never registers its outbound leg with the split plane, so every callback is
refused `extension_offline`.** `P1`, `apps/engine/src/calls/channel-orchestrator.service.ts`
`placeQueueCallbackCall` (~line 4936). `SplitPlaneMediaPort.originate` opens with
`this.require("originate", request.channelId)` (`split-plane.port.ts:649`), which throws
`the leg is not registered` unless `registerOutboundLeg` ran first. The click-to-call path does
exactly that at line 4841; the queue-callback path never calls it, for either rung. F6's plan fix is
correct and now reaches the dial — the `invalid_target` refusal simply hid this one. The engine
then maps the throw to `extension_offline`, which is a misleading name for it. Still open alongside:
**`GET /api/v1/queues/:id/callbacks` is 404** (`apps/api`), so a pending callback cannot be
inspected, and `related_call_id` cannot be observed until the callback connects.

**G4 — early media reaches the caller as signalling but never as audio: mediad does not relay
before the answer.** `P0` for a real deployment, `apps/mediad` / the engine's early-media
composition. F4 is genuinely fixed at the signalling layer — the caller gets `183` **with** the
carrier's answer, and the bus events now carry `status 183, hasEarlyMedia true` with the `sdpAnswer`
on the UAC leg only. But **0 RTP packets reach the caller for the whole 8 s of early media**, on a
non-WebRTC UA and on the browser alike, while mediad's own log shows the carrier's tone arriving at
its B-leg port. mediad's trace for the call is `session allocated (B)` → `session codec settled (B)`
→ `session allocated (A)` → _(8 s of nothing)_ → `session codec settled (B)` again at the 200 →
`sessions bridged`. **The two sessions are bridged only at the answer**, and an unbridged pair
relays nothing — so pre-answer audio has no path. The A-leg session's codec is never settled during
early media either. Audio after the 200 is fine (299 packets both ways), which also **retires
FIX-final3's WebRTC suspicion**: the final3 "no audio even after the 200" was an artefact of the
final2 fake carrier falling silent at the 200, not of `apps/web`.

**G5 — the recording indicator is now purely an `apps/web` gap.** `P2`. The engine writes
`recording` and `recording-paused` onto the live `channels` row (proved above), and the softphone
still renders neither an indicator nor a pause control. Whatever reads `flags` in the softphone
panel does not consume these two.
